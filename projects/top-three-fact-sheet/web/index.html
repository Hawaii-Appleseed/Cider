Tax Fairness Fact Sheet

Local families are leaving because they can’t afford to stay, while our tax code rewards those who profit most from our islands. Three changes ask the wealthiest to pay their fair share and keep money in working families’ pockets.

Be ready for the 2027 session. Sign up for bill alerts and testimony at hitaxfairness.org

01

Fix the Conveyance Tax

$271M

first-year revenue under last session’s House bill, rising to $303M by FY2031. Up to $60M a year for Hawaiian Home Lands.

Rates unchanged since 2009, and none of the revenue reaches Hawaiian Home Lands.

The tax jumps at set prices: about $2,000 just under $1M, $3,000 at $1M.

~30,000 beneficiaries are waiting for homestead lots.

WHAT WE SUPPORT

More from high-value and investment property sales, protection for families buying a home to live in, and new revenue for Hawaiian Home Lands.

02

Keep Working Families’ Tax Credits

$76M

less for working families in 2028 if the 2023 expansions expire. Renewing costs about $307M over 2028–2031.

Act 163 doubled the earned income and food/excise credits; both end after 2027.

About 180,000 households would receive less. Single parents lose $817 on average.

~2,100 more people, about 500 keiki, fall into poverty.

WHAT WE SUPPORT

Extend the earned income and food/excise tax credits before they expire. Details on page 2.

03

Tax Capital Gains Like Wages

$110M+

estimated new revenue each year. About 90% would come from the top 1% of households.

Gains from selling stocks and land are capped at 7.25%; wages are taxed up to 13%.

Act 24 (2026) left gains alone, so the gap grew from 3.75 to 5.75 points.

Most local families would pay nothing more.

WHAT WE SUPPORT

Tax capital gains at the same rates as ordinary income, so investment income is not taxed lower than a paycheck.

Keep Working Families’ Tax Credits

In 2023, Act 163 doubled the state earned income tax credit and the food/excise tax credit. Both expansions end after 2027, and a 2026 bill to extend them did not pass. Here is where the money goes today, and who loses if the expansions expire.

WHERE THE BENEFITS FLOW NOW

State EITC dollars by income, 2024 ($ millions)

The expansion is 55% of what these credits pay. Head-of-household filers receive 55% of EITC dollars, and 43% of all EITC dollars go to incomes of $15K–$30K.

WHO STANDS TO LOSE

Total loss by family type, 2028 ($ millions)

Single parents are hit hardest: 64% lose part of a credit, averaging $817.

WHAT WE SUPPORT

Extend the earned income and food/excise tax credits before they expire. Letting them lapse would push about 2,100 more people, including about 500 keiki, below the poverty line.
