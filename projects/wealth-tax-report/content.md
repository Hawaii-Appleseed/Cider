Wealth Tax Report

[[sources]]
[altrata-market-sizing]: Altrata (formerly Wealth-X), “Market Sizing Wealth Data for Hawaiʻi,” custom data prepared for the Institute for Policy Studies, November 9, 2025 (unpublished; on file with Hawaiʻi Appleseed). Altrata estimates the number of residents at each wealth level, and their combined net worth, with its proprietary Wealth and Investable Assets Model, which combines national economic data with its database of wealthy individuals. Its tiers are cumulative: the $20 million figures include everyone above $50 million. These data underlie Table 1 and Figures 1, 2, and 4.
[united-for-alice]: United For ALICE, “Hawaiʻi” state overview, 2024 data from the U.S. Census Bureau’s American Community Survey — https://www.unitedforalice.org/state-overview/hawaii
[dotax-income-tax-stats-2023]: Hawaiʻi Department of Taxation, “Hawaiʻi Individual Income Tax Statistics, Tax Year 2023,” Table 21, https://tax.hawaii.gov/stats/a5_1annual/a5_5ind_inc/. Filers with Hawaiʻi adjusted gross income over $400,000 reported $2.79 billion of the $3.62 billion in net long-term capital gains on resident and nonresident returns.
[census-forecaster-cap-gains]: Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice analysis using the Census-Forecaster tax model (run of September 24, 2026): net capital gains taxed at ordinary income rates under Act 24 (SB 3125 CD2), tax year 2027, Hawaiʻi residents only. About $170 million before allowing for behavioral response and about $128 million after it; roughly 88 percent comes from the top 1 percent of households. For comparison, ITEP’s earlier estimate of $85 million (tax year 2026, scored on pre-Act 24 brackets) is reported in Hawaiʻi Appleseed’s November 2025 blog post — https://hiappleseed.org/blog/hawaii-two-tier-tax-system-glaring-loophole-rich-pay-less

[dotax-itep-methods-note]: Hawaiʻi Appleseed Center for Law and Economic Justice, “DOTAX vs. ITEP on Hawaiʻi’s Income Tax Cuts,” methods note, August 2026, drawing on Karacaovali, “Legislative Update,” Hawaiʻi Tax Review Commission, May 26, 2026 (https://files.hawaii.gov/tax/stats/trc/docs2025/2026-05-Karacaovali_Presentation_TRC_Legislative_Update.pdf), and ITEP, “HI SB 3125 Analysis,” May 13, 2026. DOTAX’s figure ($1.453 billion in fiscal 2032, tax year 2031) is Act 46’s full annual cost against pre-Act 46 law, of which about $740 million is already being lost; ITEP’s $705 million measures only the further rise from 2026 policy to tax year 2031. The two agree to within about 1 percent.
[karacaovali-slides]: Karacaovali, “Legislative Update,” cited above, slides 14–15: Act 46’s general fund cost of $1,453.2 million in fiscal 2032 (tax year 2031), less Act 24’s estimated $297.3 million in new revenue that year from its bracket changes and tax credit sunsets, leaves a net loss of about $1.16 billion.
